# What Great Front-of-House Workers Have in Common

Front of house (FOH) is the part of the restaurant guests see: hosts, servers, bartenders, bussers, food runners, and managers. The kitchen makes the food, but FOH makes the experience.

The best FOH workers aren't just friendly. They're organized, calm under pressure, and always two steps ahead. Every one of those skills can be learned.

## The 12 core skills

| Skill | Why it matters |
| 1. Hospitality mindset | Guests remember how you made them feel more than what they ate |
| 2. Communication | Clear words with guests, kitchen, and team prevent mistakes |
| 3. Menu knowledge | Confident recommendations increase sales and trust |
| 4. Multitasking and prioritizing | Busy shifts require juggling many tables at once |
| 5. Time management | Timing courses, checks, and side work keeps the room running |
| 6. Memory and attention to detail | Orders, allergies, and preferences must be right |
| 7. Teamwork | Restaurants run on people helping each other |
| 8. Problem-solving | Things go wrong every shift; how you fix them matters |
| 9. Staying calm under pressure | Stress spreads, and so does calm |
| 10. Food safety and allergy awareness | Guest health depends on it |
| 11. Responsible alcohol service | Protects guests, you, and the restaurant's license |
| 12. POS and cash handling | Accuracy protects your money and your job |

# The Hospitality Mindset

Service is doing your job. Hospitality is making people feel cared for while you do it.

## Service vs. hospitality

| Service | Hospitality |
| Bringing the food on time | Noticing a guest is cold and offering a table away from the door |
| Taking the order correctly | Remembering a regular's usual and asking about their kid's game |
| Answering questions | Offering a sample when a guest can't decide |
| Dropping the check | Thanking the guest by name and inviting them back |

## Habits of hospitality

- **Make eye contact and smile** at every guest you pass, not just the ones in your section.
- **Say yes when you can.** Look for ways to meet requests before saying no.
- **Own the guest's experience.** If you see a problem at any table, help or get help.
- **Stay genuine.** Guests can tell when you're following a script without meaning it.

# The Steps of Service

Most restaurants follow a version of these steps. Learn your restaurant's specific standards, but this framework works almost anywhere.

1. **Greet** within 60 seconds of seating. Introduce yourself and offer drinks.
2. **Deliver drinks** and take appetizer orders. Share specials.
3. **Take the order.** Repeat back tricky orders, and ask about allergies.
4. **Enter the order** into the POS right away, with accurate modifiers.
5. **Deliver food** with a description ("Here's your salmon with lemon butter"), and place plates in the right position.
6. **Check back** within two bites or two minutes.
7. **Maintain the table:** refills, clearing, fresh silverware.
8. **Offer dessert and after-dinner drinks.**
9. **Present the check** at the right time, and process payment promptly.
10. **Thank and farewell:** invite guests back.

## Table maintenance standards

[ ] Water glasses refilled before they're half empty
[ ] Empty glasses and plates cleared promptly (once everyone at the table is finished)
[ ] Used silverware replaced before the next course
[ ] Crumbs and spills cleaned between courses
[ ] Condiments and extra napkins brought before the guest needs to ask
[ ] Table is never left without a check-in for too long

# Communication Skills

## With guests

- **Speak clearly** and adjust your volume to the room.
- **Listen fully** before answering. Repeat back orders with modifications.
- **Use positive language:** say "What I can do is..." instead of "We can't."
- **Read body language:** crossed arms, looking around, or pushed-away plates all tell you something.

## With the kitchen

- Enter orders accurately and completely. The kitchen can only make what's on the ticket.
- Use your restaurant's terms ("on the fly," "86'd," "all day," "behind") correctly.
- Communicate allergies clearly and directly, in person as well as in the system when that's your restaurant's policy.
- Be respectful, even when things are hectic. The kitchen is your partner.

## With your team

- Call out "behind," "corner," and "hot" when moving through the restaurant.
- Tell teammates when you need help, and offer help when you see someone drowning.
- Hand off tables with clear notes: what's been ordered, what's pending, any special requests.

## Common restaurant terms

| Term | Meaning |
| 86'd | Out of an item; it's no longer available |
| On the fly | Needed as fast as possible |
| All day | Total count of an item needed across all orders |
| In the weeds | Overwhelmed and behind |
| Behind / Corner / Hot | Safety call-outs to avoid collisions |
| Campers | Guests who stay long after finishing |
| Comp | An item given for free |
| Double / Clopen | Two shifts in one day / closing then opening the next morning |
| Expo | The person or station that checks and sends food out |
| Top | Number of guests at a table ("a four-top") |

# Menu Knowledge and Selling

## Know every item

- Main ingredients and preparation (fried, grilled, raw, etc.)
- Common allergens (see the table in the next chapter)
- Portion sizes and what's good to share
- What can and can't be modified
- Two or three descriptive words for every item

## Know the drinks

- House cocktails and what's in them
- Beer on tap and what styles they are
- Wines by the glass and what they pair with
- Non-alcoholic options and mocktails

> Make menu flashcards. Put the dish name on one side and ingredients, allergens, and your go-to description on the other. Study five a day.

For upselling scripts and more sales techniques, see Guide 01 in this series.

# Food Safety and Allergy Awareness

## Allergy awareness is non-negotiable

Food allergies can be life-threatening. In the U.S., the major food allergens are:

| Major allergen | Common sources on menus |
| Milk | Butter, cheese, cream sauces, some breads |
| Eggs | Aioli, mayonnaise, batters, pasta, baked goods |
| Fish | Fish sauce, Caesar dressing, Worcestershire |
| Shellfish | Shrimp, crab, lobster, some stocks |
| Tree nuts | Pesto, desserts, some sauces and oils |
| Peanuts | Sauces, dressings, desserts, fryer oils |
| Wheat | Breads, batters, pasta, soy sauce, thickened sauces |
| Soy | Soy sauce, tofu, many processed ingredients |
| Sesame | Buns, dressings, tahini, hummus |

## The allergy protocol

1. **Ask** every table if anyone has allergies.
2. **Listen** carefully and write it down.
3. **Check** with the kitchen or manager. Never guess about ingredients.
4. **Communicate** the allergy clearly on the ticket and in person, following restaurant policy.
5. **Deliver** allergy meals separately if you can, confirming with the guest.

! Never tell a guest a dish is "safe" if you aren't completely sure. "Let me check with the kitchen" is always the right answer.

## Basic food safety for FOH

- Wash your hands often, especially after clearing plates, handling cash, or touching your face or phone.
- Hold plates by the edges and glasses by the stem or base, never by the rim.
- Don't touch ice or ready-to-eat food with bare hands; use scoops and tongs.
- Report spills immediately and clean them up to prevent slips.

# Responsible Alcohol Service

If you serve alcohol, you're responsible for serving it legally and safely.

- **Check ID** for anyone who looks under the age your restaurant's policy sets (often 30 or 40). Know what a valid ID looks like in your state.
- **Watch for signs of intoxication:** slurred speech, loud or aggressive behavior, stumbling, spilling drinks.
- **Slow down service** by offering water and food, and taking longer between rounds.
- **Involve a manager** before cutting someone off, and do it privately and respectfully.
- **Never serve** a visibly intoxicated guest or a minor.

Many states require or encourage alcohol server training such as ServSafe Alcohol or TIPS. It's a valuable certification even if your state doesn't require it.

# Handling Difficult Situations

## Difficult guests

Use the **HEARD** method:

- **Hear** the guest out fully without interrupting.
- **Empathize:** "I understand how frustrating that must be."
- **Apologize** sincerely, even if it isn't your fault.
- **Resolve** the issue quickly, or get a manager.
- **Diagnose** afterwards: what caused it, and how can you prevent it next time?

## When to get a manager right away

- A guest is aggressive, threatening, or harassing you or others
- There's a food allergy reaction or medical emergency
- A guest wants something only a manager can approve (large comps, policy exceptions)
- A guest is visibly intoxicated or a minor is trying to order alcohol
- You feel unsafe for any reason

! You never have to tolerate harassment or threats from guests. Tell a manager. If you're being mistreated by management, see Guide 03.

## Staying calm under pressure

- **Breathe:** a slow breath in for 4 counts and out for 6 resets your nervous system in seconds.
- **Prioritize:** use the priority order from Guide 01 to decide what's next.
- **Ask for help early,** before you're drowning.
- **Take it one table at a time.** You can't fix the whole room at once.

# POS, Payments, and Cash Handling

- Enter orders and modifiers carefully. Double-check before you send.
- Never leave your server login open. Log out every time.
- Count your cash bank at the start and end of each shift.
- Present change and receipts accurately and politely.
- Follow your restaurant's rules for voids and comps. Always get approval.
- Keep your own record of tips and sales (see the tracker in Guide 01).

# Growing Your Career

FOH skills can lead to many roles:

| Role | Skills to build |
| Host → Server | Menu knowledge, table management, sales |
| Server → Bartender | Drink knowledge, speed, multitasking |
| Server → Trainer | Communication, patience, service standards |
| Trainer → Shift lead / Supervisor | Leadership, conflict resolution, scheduling |
| Supervisor → Manager | Finances, hiring, operations, labor laws |

## Certifications that add value

- **Food handler card** (required in many states and counties)
- **ServSafe Alcohol** or **TIPS** (responsible alcohol service)
- **ServSafe Food Protection Manager** (for supervisory roles)
- **Wine and spirits courses** (such as WSET Level 1 or introductory sommelier courses)
